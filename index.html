📚 Multi-System Pathology Reference

Evidence-based clinical guidelines, physiological etiologies, and emergency red flags across 6 key medical domains.

🫁 Respiratory & Pulmonary

Infections, airway obstruction, and alveolar gas exchange pathology.

  • Viral URI vs. Influenza: Colds develop gradually; Flu presents with abrupt prostration and high fever.
  • Acute Bronchitis: Lower airway inflammation; cough with clear/yellow sputum often persisting 2–3 weeks.
  • Bronchospasm: Expiratory wheeze with prolonged expiratory phase.
  • Alveolar Threshold: Resting pulse oximetry below 95% indicates compromised gas exchange.
❤️ Cardiovascular & Circulatory

Myocardial ischemia, rhythm irregularities, and vascular dynamics.

  • Sinus Tachycardia: Rate >100 BPM secondary to pyrexia, fluid deficit, or adrenergic surge.
  • Orthostatic POTS: Pulse jump >30 BPM upon standing without sustained hypotensive drop.
  • Hypertensive Escalation: SBP >140 mmHg requires resting re-check after 15 minutes.
🤢 Gastrointestinal & Hepatic

Enteric mucosal inflammation, motility disturbances, and fluid losses.

  • Viral Gastroenteritis: Noro/rotavirus with rapid fluid shifts, resolving in 24–72 hours.
  • GERD / Acid Pyrosis: Retrosternal burning exacerbated by supine posture post-prandially.
  • Functional IBS: Recurrent abdominal pain linked to defecation and bowel habit changes.
  • Electrolyte Replacement: WHO ORS ratio balances glucose-sodium intestinal cotransport.
🧠 Neurological & Cranial

Cephalea classification, vestibulopathies, and sensory disturbances.

  • Tension Cephalea: Bilateral 'tight band' pressure with myofascial neck tenderness.
  • Vascular Migraine: Unilateral throbbing, nausea, light sensitivity, preceded by visual aura.
  • BPPV Vertigo: Semicircular canal otolith displacement triggered by head turning.
🦴 Musculoskeletal & Rheumatic

Ligamentous strains, articular degeneration, and myalgias.

  • Lumbosacral Strain: Paraspinal muscle spasms following torsion or lifting without radiculopathy.
  • Joint Arthralgia: Morning stiffness (<30 mins for OA, >1 hr for inflammatory/RA).
  • Overuse Tendinitis: Micro-tearing of collagen bundles with pinpoint tendon tenderness.
🩹 Dermatological & Allergic

Epidermal hypersensitivity, urticaria, and infectious exanthems.

  • Acute Urticaria: Mast cell degranulation causing pruritic transient wheals.
  • Contact Dermatitis: Type IV delayed hypersensitivity to nickel, perfumes, or botanicals.
  • Herpes Zoster: Unilateral dermatomal nerve pain followed by grouped vesicular clusters.

⚡ Rapid Action First-Aid Life Support Protocols

❤️ Suspected Heart Attack / Thoracic Pain
  • 1 Immediately dial 108 / 112. Do not drive yourself.
  • 2 Sit semi-recumbent (knees bent) to relieve myocardial cardiac load.
  • 3 If not allergic, chew one full Aspirin (300-325mg) with water.
  • 4 Loosen tight clothing around neck and waist; maintain calm ventilation.
🫁 Acute Airway Obstruction / Choking
  • 1 Encourage patient to cough forcefully if partially breathing.
  • 2 Stand behind, deliver 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades.
  • 3 Perform 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver) inward and upward.
  • 4 If patient loses consciousness, lower to floor and initiate CPR (100-120 bpm).
🧠 Stroke F.A.S.T. Assessment Matrix
  • F Face Drooping: Ask person to smile; check for asymmetrical facial droop.
  • A Arm Weakness: Ask to raise both arms; check if one arm drifts down.
  • S Speech Difficulty: Ask to repeat a simple sentence; note slurring or aphasia.
  • T Time to Call 112: Record exact time of symptom onset for tPA thrombolytic window.
⚡ Anaphylaxis Severe Allergic Reaction
  • 1 Administer Epinephrine Auto-Injector (EpiPen) into mid-outer thigh.
  • 2 Keep injector pressed firmly for 5-10 seconds; massage site.
  • 3 Lay patient flat with legs elevated unless severe vomiting or breathing distress.
  • 4 Dial 108 / 112 immediately as a second biphasic reaction may occur.
🌡️ Severe Fever & Febrile Convulsions
  • 1 Gently position individual on their side in the Recovery Position.
  • 2 Clear surrounding area of sharp objects; cushion the head gently.
  • 3 NEVER insert anything into the mouth or restrain convulsive limbs.
  • 4 Time the seizure; if lasting >5 minutes, summon emergency ambulance immediately.
